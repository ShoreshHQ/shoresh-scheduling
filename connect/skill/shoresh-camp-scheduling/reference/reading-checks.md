# Reading a week's checks (the Why list)

`read_week_checks` gives exactly what the counter beside the grid shows (API-1.30): "29 of 35
placed", what is still needed, priorities met, and its lines:

- **Open:** a cell nothing fills, each with one reason (CORE-6.2).
- **Still needed / under:** a group short of an activity's minimum this week.
- **Over:** past an activity's maximum. The maximum may be exceeded, and it is shown (CORE-4.7).
- **To look at:** clashes; a staff member leading two activities at once (CORE-6.20); a group
  changing area of camp often in a day (CORE-6.21); setup changed since; rain plan full.
- **Notes:** such as a blank groups fit read as 1.

## How to pass them on

- Report the lines as they are, in their words. Never add a problem Shoresh didn't list, and never
  call a week clean without reading its checks: a schedule read holds none (API-1.27).
- Offer the next step; don't take it. "Archery: Bunk 4 still needed 1" becomes "Bunk 4 is one
  Archery short this week. Wednesday Period 2 and Thursday Period 4 are Open. Want me to put it in
  one?" Never quietly lower Archery's minimum or change other setup to make a line go away.
- **Accepting a line** (`accept_line`) is the person's call. Accept only the line they named, in the
  words the checks gave. An accepted line comes back if it gets worse (CORE-6.8).
- Every conflict is shown, never resolved silently (CORE-6.1). A conflict a person's hand edit
  made stays theirs to keep or change (CORE-4.8).

## Why is Swim on Tuesday?

The checks explain Open cells and problems, not why the build chose each place. In general a build
lays down locks, hand edits and set times first, then minimums, then High priority before Low
(CORE-5.1, CORE-5.2, CORE-5.3). Say that much, and no more.
